1. Problem Statement & Opportunity Statement

1.1 Context & Core Customer Problem

In traditional Indian agribusiness marketing, smallholder farmers operate in an environment characterized by severe structural information asymmetry, lack of pre-harvest visibility, and visual inspection exploitation. For an FPO representing 180 smallholder members generating approximately 40 harvest batches weekly, bringing perishable produce to terminal Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) mandis creates acute vulnerability.

The Mandi Squeeze: Commission agents (*kaccha arhatiyas*) exploit subjective visual grading, non-standardized weight deductions (*kata chhoot* of 3–5%), and perishable shelf-life urgency to impose a 15% to 30% price reduction on uncommitted produce arriving at mandi docks.

1.2 Clear Problem Statement

"Smallholder farmers belonging to the 180-member FPO suffer from 18–28% price realization loss due to post-harvest distress selling at local mandis, caused by buyers’ inability to visually inspect crop quality, ascertain standardized grades, and verify exact harvest availability dates prior to harvest."

1.3 Clear Opportunity Statement

"To establish a standardized, weekly digital photo-catalogue and pre-harvest booking protocol for 40 weekly harvest entries, enabling verified visual grading, scheduled farmgate collection, and direct digital linkage with urban B2B procurement partners, thereby capturing a 20%+ price premium for farmers while delivering fresh, traceable produce to institutional buyers within 24 hours of harvest."

4. End-to-End Operating Journey & Service Touchpoints

To ensure data precision across all 40 weekly listings, the FPO operates a standardized 5-stage protocol:

Stage 1: Pre-Harvest Listing (T - 72h)

Farmer logs harvest intent on WhatsApp. Village Scout visits field, verifies maturity with digital refractometer, and captures 3 standardized photos using the FPO reference scale card.

Stage 2: FPO Quality Audit (T - 48h)

FPO Quality Agronomist inspects listings on the admin dashboard against AGMARK tolerances. Approved lots receive unique Batch IDs (e.g. LOT-TOM-A-042).

Stage 3: Catalogue Broadcast & Booking (T - 36h)

Interactive web catalogue published. Automated WhatsApp broadcast sent to 120+ registered procurement managers every Monday & Thursday at 7:00 AM. Buyers pre-book lots with 20% escrow token.

Stage 4: Synchronized Harvest & Aggregation (T - 18h to T - 6h)

Farmers harvest at optimal dawn hours. Produce assembled in reusable ventilated HDPE crates at 3 village collection centers. Digital platform weighment recorded.

Stage 5: Direct Dispatch & T+24h Settlement (T + 0h to T + 24h)

Light Commercial Vehicles (LCVs) deliver straight to buyer distribution centers. Buyer scans batch QR code and signs digital Goods Received Note (GRN). Automated UPI payment credited to farmer within 24 hours.
